# Running Coach Glossary

This file is the single source of truth for all training jargon used in this plugin. Any inline glosses baked into templates should match the phrasing here exactly (≤ 1-word variation allowed for readability in context).

---

## Paces (Daniels labels)

- **E (Easy)** — conversational running pace; you can chat in full sentences.
- **M (Marathon)** — the pace you'd hold for a full marathon; comfortably steady, not casual.
- **T (Threshold)** — "comfortably hard"; you can speak a few words but not a full sentence.
- **I (Interval)** — hard reps of 3–5 min at roughly 5K race effort.
- **R (Repetition)** — short, fast strides at roughly mile race effort with full recovery between.
- **L (Long run)** — the week's longest run, usually at Easy pace.

---

## Effort & Physiology

- **RPE** — Rate of Perceived Exertion: 1 = walking, 10 = all-out sprint.
- **HR drift** — heart rate creeping up during a steady-effort run even though pace stays constant — a fatigue or heat signal.
- **Zone 1–2** — the easy-to-moderate heart-rate range where aerobic fitness is built.
- **Aerobic base** — the long-term capacity built by lots of easy running; the foundation everything else sits on.

---

## Workout Structure

- **Strides** — 4–6 fast (not sprint) 20-second pickups at the end of an easy run; primes leg turnover.
- **Shakeout** — a short, easy pre-race jog to loosen up without fatiguing the legs.
- **Quality session** — a workout day with a target intensity (T, I, R, or M), as opposed to an easy day.
- **Polarized 80/20** — roughly 80% of weekly mileage at easy effort, ~20% hard; almost nothing in between.

---

## Plan Structure

- **Macrocycle** — the full training block leading up to a goal race (e.g., 16 weeks).
The four phases of a macrocycle, in order:
- **Base** — pile up easy miles to build aerobic foundation before adding intensity.
- **Build** — add quality sessions (T, I, R) on top of the aerobic base.
- **Peak** — race-specific work at highest volume and intensity; brief and demanding.
- **Taper** — cut volume before race day while maintaining intensity.
- **Recovery week** — a planned lighter week (~70% of normal volume) to absorb training and avoid overreach.

---

## Adherence & Adaptation

- **VDOT** — Jack Daniels' single-number fitness score, derived from a recent race or hard effort, that drives all your training paces.
- **VDOT bump** — your fitness score moved up one step; training paces will be slightly faster going forward.
- **Strike rules** — the rule of thumb for when to step down (3 misses → recovery week), hold, or advance (3 strong workouts → VDOT bump).
- **Completion %** — how much of the prescribed workout duration you actually finished.
- **Pace delta** — how far off (faster or slower) you ran versus the prescribed pace.
- **Verdict** — the workout's outcome tag: on-target, under (easier than prescribed), over (harder), or aborted.
